Built from what people told us

Every one of these came from someone who quit the other apps. Each has a mechanism behind it, not a promise.

1 “You scroll for hours.”

A drop, not a feed

Sixty pieces on Sunday, then it closes. You can finish it in ten minutes. The cap is also what stops us lowering the bar to grow, which is how every other marketplace ends up full of junk.

2 “So much Shein and Cotton On.”

The walk-down-the-road rule

If you could buy it new nearby for a similar price, it does not come in. That is not a brand blacklist, it is a test any seller can apply to their own pile without being told what is good taste.

3 “Sizing.”

Measured, never labelled

Flat measurements in centimetres on every piece, scored against your saved fit profile. A size 16 from 1975 is close to a modern 10. The label is a brand's opinion. The tape is not.

4 “Girls shape dresses on the floor to fake the fit.”

The hem gives it away

Same waist, opposite garment. We measure the hem as well, so the shape badge comes from a ratio instead of a photo. You can arrange a dress however you like on your lounge floor, the numbers still say what it is.

5 “Sellers reply in weeks, or never.”

Nothing left to ask

Measurements, shape, condition and flaws are on the listing, the price is fixed and stock is one of one. Almost every message sent on the other apps only exists because the listing was incomplete. Complete the listing and the message disappears.

6 Stock nobody wants sits on the other apps for years.

Nothing goes stale

Six months unsold and a piece delists itself, straight back to the seller's drafts. They can relist it, drop the price, or admit it belongs somewhere else. You get a warning badge for the last four weeks, so a thing you have been watching never just vanishes on you.

How a listing gets checked

Sellers do not start from a blank form. We match the piece against a local reference of known styles, pre-fill what it should measure, and flag anything that disagrees. Pick a piece and type a measurement to watch it run.

Try a number a few cm off and watch it flag

What this is. A working prototype. The 60 garments are sample stock with realistic flat measurements. Every fit verdict, shape badge, reference check, search result and count on this page is computed live from that data, not written by hand.

It runs on arithmetic. No model is called at any point, including the search. Fit is a comparison of centimetres against tolerances, shape is a hem-to-waist ratio, search is a local parser over the stock, and the listing check is a lookup against a reference table. The whole thing costs nothing per view.
